# Visual Direction

Choose a visual idea that follows from the product's actual purpose, content, and use. It must guide visible decisions well enough to reject an attractive but unsuitable option.

For a new concept or substantial redesign, load [brief to design decisions](references/brief-to-design-decisions.md) to select a direction with representative content, test the largest uncertainty, and carry a usable specification into implementation. A local refinement can use the principles below directly.

When choosing among visual styles or developing color/theme and typography variations, read [visual languages and themes](references/visual-languages-and-themes.md). Resolve those choices from the actual content and audience, then carry the selected system through ordinary UI states.

## Find the product's visual opportunity

For premium, luxury or editorial briefs, load [luxury editorial direction](references/luxury-editorial-direction.md) to resolve subject, typography, palette, imagery, motion character and finish from the product's evidence. Apply it to the actual project rather than importing a page template.

Establish who is using the product, what they are trying to understand or accomplish, how often they return, and what uncertainty the experience should remove. Identify the primary action or viewing question separately from secondary exploration and commercial goals.

Ask what this product can show or do that an unrelated product cannot. Useful sources include the structure of its information, the behavior of its objects, authentic imagery, a distinctive comparison, or the sequence through which knowledge is revealed. Do not invent endorsements, urgency, precision, or outcomes to create authority.

Translate an emotional target into visible relationships and behavior. Calm may mean stable anchors and restrained status changes. Precision may mean aligned values, explicit units, and exact object connections. Playfulness may mean reversible exploration with legible consequences. A request for luxury, beauty, or boldness still needs a product-specific subject and compositional idea; those adjectives do not select a palette or typeface.

For open identity work, compare distinct directions where the choice is consequential. Change structure, image treatment, typography, or behavior enough that the options imply different experiences; palette swaps alone rarely resolve an art-direction question. Judge them with the actual primary subject, representative title, and action in view, accounting for asset availability, accessibility, and rendering cost. Select a coherent direction; mixing unrelated signatures usually destroys the reason each works.

## Make the thesis operative

Express the product, visual organizing idea, and user benefit in one sentence. For a collection of restored instruments, a plausible direction is an inspection catalog in which instrument form leads and provenance remains adjacent. Its implications are subject-led framing, precise captions, useful detail views, and quiet working controls.

Choose where expression is concentrated and where consistency carries the task. A distinctive crop, oversized specimen, editorial title, spatial relationship, or interaction can establish identity while familiar controls support it. Carry the governing relationship into ordinary content and later states; a dramatic opening followed by unrelated default sections does not sustain a direction.

Resolve the following relationships at the depth needed by the task:

| Choice | Decision it must settle |
| --- | --- |
| Focal hierarchy | What is noticed first, understood second, and acted on next? |
| Typography character | Does expression belong in titles, reading, labels, or nowhere? |
| Density and rhythm | Which information must remain comparable; where does a pause help? |
| Dominant forms and balance | Do rails, repeated modules, asymmetry, or continuous flow express the content? |
| Color and surface | Which roles express identity, status, grouping, and interaction? |
| Depth | Which overlaps or spatial relationships carry meaning? |
| Imagery and geometry | What authentic subject, crop, silhouette, or construction identifies the product? |
| Movement | What character belongs to response, navigation, manipulation, and optional narrative? |
| Restraint | Which regions need visual quiet so the important subject can lead? |

Proceed to [composition](../visual-composition/SKILL.md) when these choices need spatial expression. Use [storytelling](../visual-storytelling/SKILL.md) when the organizing idea depends on a sequence.

Carry forward the thesis, recognizable visual relationship, emphasis order, expressive and quiet regions, and boundaries on change. Make each choice concrete enough to settle later crops, type treatments, layouts, and effects without reopening the whole brief.

## Refine an established identity

Inspect representative normal, dense, exceptional, and small-space views. Separate consistent intentional rules from accidental inconsistency. Assess typography, spacing, shape and corner treatment, color relationships, surfaces, depth, imagery, iconography, navigation, density, and motion.

Classify proposed changes as keep, refine, remove, or introduce. Preserve recognizable relationships that still support the task. Refine a weak expression of a good rule before replacing the rule. Introduce a new visual device only where the current vocabulary cannot express a needed distinction. Do not infer permission to rebrand from a request to improve polish.

## Detect unjustified visual devices

Examine repeated cards, rounded containers, pills, badges, centered sections, oversized headings, excessive empty space, tiny captions, arbitrary gradients, universal glow, blur, transparency, particles, floating objects, and animated backgrounds. These are materials, not prohibited styles.

For each prominent device, name its contribution to hierarchy, identity, comprehension, feedback, storytelling, focus, or atmosphere. Then consider what happens if it is removed. Keep a bold device when it creates the intended identity or emotional force, and quiet nearby competitors. If its removal preserves that effect and improves clarity, reduce it. If every section uses the same emphasis, restore differences in information role before adding new decoration.

Imagine replacing the brand name with an unrelated product. If the experience still reads identically, look for missing product-specific structure. Conventional controls may rightly remain familiar; distinctiveness need not alter how an ordinary action works.

## Acceptance and repair

The rendered experience should express the thesis through its subject, hierarchy, and behavior. If the first view does not reveal what the product offers, repair subject and content priority before decorating the introduction. If expression disappears beyond the opening, carry a defining relationship into ordinary views. If every element is equally expressive, strengthen the intended focal device and reduce competing emphasis. Unreadable controls need a quieter task region; a redesign that has lost recognition needs recovery of its successful visual relationships.
