# From Product Brief to Implementable Art Direction

Use this reference when the desired identity is open, a redesign needs a governing idea, or several attractive directions compete. Keep the decision record proportional to the task. A short product page may need a paragraph and one representative composition; a substantial product may need several states.

## Recover the brief from evidence

Inspect the actual product, content, assets, and existing interface before making a style choice. Establish:

| Question | Evidence to carry forward |
| --- | --- |
| Who arrives, and why? | One primary task and the uncertainty preventing it |
| What distinguishes this product? | A real object, workflow, result, collection, or relationship that can be shown |
| What must users decide? | The facts, comparison, controls, and next action supporting that decision |
| What is already fixed? | Identity, supplied copy, asset rights, navigation conventions, technical constraints |
| What can be explored? | Composition, imagery, motion, density, type expression, spatial presentation |
| What is unavailable? | Missing assets or facts that change the plausible direction |

Retrieve answers from the project when possible. Mark unresolved assumptions and proceed with a reversible interpretation. A missing critical product fact merits a focused question; unknown margin values do not.

Use the real title, representative long label, one ordinary content block, and actual main action in early compositions. A visual concept that depends on a two-word placeholder heading has not yet survived the product.

## Compare directions by their consequences

The following is an illustrative brief, not a claim about a real business:

> A repairable task lamp lets buyers inspect its joints, compare two reach options, choose a finish, and find replacement parts. Supplied assets include orthographic drawings, product photographs, dimensions, and a materials list. The primary action is “Choose your configuration.”

Two plausible directions lead to different work:

| Direction | Visible organizing idea | Useful consequence | Main uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Workshop specimen | Large articulated lamp beside a precise dimension rail; finish swatches and parts stay near the object | Construction and adjustability become tangible; comparison has a stable scale | Can the available geometry communicate the joints convincingly? |
| Light in use | Photographs organized by working situation; pools of light establish section rhythm | Buyers can imagine placement and atmosphere | Are supplied photographs varied and truthful enough to carry the narrative? |

Choose using relevance to the buying question, distinguishing evidence, asset feasibility, legibility, interaction cost, and delivery constraints. Do not collapse the decision into arbitrary scores that imply measured certainty. Record the decisive evidence: “The brief prioritizes reach and serviceability, and measured drawings exist; the specimen direction can show both directly.”

If photographs are the only reliable object evidence, keep them central while an optional model remains a labeled interpretation. A sophisticated rendering cannot establish unsupported product dimensions.

## Turn the direction into commitments

A useful thesis names subject, relationship, and benefit:

> Present the lamp as an inspectable instrument, keeping its reach, joints, and available replacements beside the current configuration so buyers can understand how it will work over time.

Turn that sentence into a compact working specification. For this example:

```yaml
subject: current lamp configuration
primary_action: Choose your configuration
evidence: [reach dimensions, available finishes, replaceable parts]
focal_order: [lamp silhouette, product promise, configuration controls]
signature: dimension rail aligned with the lamp's working plane
expressive_regions: [opening object view, joint inspection]
quiet_regions: [specifications, pricing, purchase controls, errors]
type_roles: {display: product character, reading: explanation, numeric: dimensions}
color_roles: {accent: selected configuration and primary action, status: independent}
movement: direct articulation on input; stable object while reading specifications
responsive_invariants: [selected finish, active part, readable dimensions, reachable action]
assumptions: [joint internals are inferred until confirmed]
```

This is an example of information to preserve, not a required schema. Reuse the host project's tokens and state names when available. Record decisions that another specialist needs; omit decorative prose.

Assign a small set of visible relationships to carry identity through ordinary content. Here, the dimension rail can align specification rows and captions; a focused joint view can explain repair; the finish accent can connect swatches with the selected material. Repeating an opening animation in every section would consume attention without deepening the concept.

## Test the most consequential uncertainty early

Build a representative slice that contains the defining subject, real heading, one ordinary information region, and a working action. Include a constrained layout and one focus, loading, or error state if these materially change the composition.

For the lamp example, test whether the joint remains understandable in the actual camera view and whether dimensions stay associated with the current pose. If that fails, repair object structure, view, or annotation before completing a long page around it.

Review the slice against concrete questions:

- Can a new visitor identify the product and next useful action without waiting for a sequence?
- Does the distinctive device expose information or create a memorable product-specific relationship?
- Does the treatment survive a long title, a missing image, and a normal specification block?
- Is the action still clear when pointer hover and expressive movement are unavailable?
- Are the required assets and interactions plausible within the project?

Use the outcome to approve the direction for implementation, revise a specific relationship, or choose the other direction. “Approved” here means the current design satisfies the brief's acceptance criteria; it does not substitute for user approval where the task explicitly requires it.

## Carry the concept through delivery

Build the full requested flow, then inspect the opening, an ordinary middle region, the main task, and the final outcome together. Repair identity drift at the shared rule: type hierarchy, image treatment, alignment rail, surface roles, or interaction language.

Record the thesis, implemented signature, unresolved assumptions, and the evidence used to accept the result. Describe observed quality rather than claiming an award, market valuation, or equivalence to a specific model.
