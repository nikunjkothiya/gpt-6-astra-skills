# Visual QA

Judge the rendered experience against product intent and delivery conditions. Successful construction, attractive source structure, or a captured image that has not been viewed cannot establish visual quality.

For reference matching, difficult visual regressions, or motion with interruption and return behavior, load [render and playback checks](references/render-and-playback.md). For a substantial final review or comparison between implementations, load [quality evidence and acceptance](references/quality-evidence.md). Use the relevant procedure; a local spacing repair does not require the full evaluation protocol.

## Choose evidence for the artifact

For a local visual repair, inspect the affected relationship and adjacent conditions that share it. For a substantial experience, inspect the main journey, relevant states, and meaningfully different compositions. Do not require unrelated disciplines for a narrow change.

For an interface, observe the primary action, feedback, recovery, important overlays, and relevant input or content variation. Inspect both the initial view and broader content structure where they serve different roles.

For an object, inspect silhouette, major proportions, attachments, thickness, surface continuity, required views, and articulated extremes. For a delivered asset, observe its appearance in the intended presentation where available; an authoring preview does not prove parity.

For motion, observe playback, onset, sequencing, interruption, reversal, settling, and loop boundaries where applicable. Endpoint stills do not reveal stutter, popping, velocity discontinuities, or disorientation.

Use normal viewing size for readability, reduced scale for overall composition, and close inspection for diagnosis. Fine detail visible only at extreme enlargement should not outrank a weakness in the actual presentation.

## Judge craft beyond absence of defects

Compare the intended attention order with the visible one. Identify the first subject, the next useful information, and where the eye gets trapped or diverted. A page can have no clipping and still fail because every region carries equal weight; an object can have valid geometry and still lack a readable profile.

Examine precision at visible joins: text and icon alignment, image-caption relationships, transitions between dense and quiet regions, material boundaries, contact points, moving attachments, and the moment control changes hands. These relationships often reveal unfinished craft more clearly than isolated polished elements.

Ask whether the product's chosen visual signature appears strongly enough to be perceived at normal size. If the result is accurate but anonymous, route back to direction or composition. If it is memorable but confusing, preserve the useful signature while simplifying competing emphasis. Do not reward decoration count, extreme minimalism, or conformity to one aesthetic.

## Use a diagnostic rubric

For each applicable dimension, identify whether it meets the intended outcome in observed conditions, needs repair, blocks the task, or remains unobserved. Mark deliberately unused capabilities as not applicable; unnecessary motion or spatial detail need not be introduced to complete the rubric.

| Dimension | Observable question |
| --- | --- |
| Product alignment | Does the visual experience serve the user's primary action or viewing question? |
| Concept clarity | Does the organizing idea appear in the subject and behavior? |
| Composition | Do focal balance, alignment, negative space, and grouping work as a whole? |
| Hierarchy | Does perceived priority agree with information importance? |
| Typography | Are roles, wrapping, measure, numbers, and enlarged text readable? |
| Spacing and density | Do gaps encode relationships and preserve useful comparison? |
| Visual identity | Are recognizable choices coherent and appropriate to the product? |
| Interaction | Do affordance, input, state, feedback, recovery, and stable anchors agree? |
| Motion | Do causality, timing, trajectory, interruption, and settling remain legible? |
| Geometry | Do silhouette, proportions, structure, thickness, and connections hold? |
| Materials | Does surface identity survive required scale and viewing conditions? |
| Lighting | Are form, separation, grounding, and focal contrast readable? |
| Camera | Do framing, perspective, control limits, and movement reveal the needed relationship? |
| Rendering | Are edges, shadows, color, transparency, and temporal output coherent? |
| Responsiveness | Does recomposition preserve the primary task, subject, and information? |
| Accessibility | Can the task and meaning survive required input and sensory alternatives? |
| Performance awareness | Does observed response and quality fit the intended conditions? |
| Originality | Do distinctive decisions arise from content and product meaning? |
| Restraint | Does each prominent effect earn its attention and cost? |
| Implementation coherence | Do actual behavior, identity, values, configuration, and presentation remain consistent? |

Scores, if useful to the project, remain diagnostic judgments. A blocked primary action or false state cannot be offset by strengths elsewhere, and an unobserved dimension is not a pass.

## Turn critique into a correction

For each significant weakness, identify the view or moment, visible problem, task impact, likely cause, highest-impact correction, and observation needed to establish improvement. Distinguish the seen symptom from the inferred cause. Name the relationship and expected change; "improve polish" is not an actionable finding.

Prioritize blocked functionality and incorrect state, then structural and access failures, then composition, scale, proportion, hierarchy, interaction continuity, motion, geometry detail, illumination, materials, and finish. Adjust by task: silhouette can be the central functional requirement of an object study, while text readability can outrank visual identity in a reading tool.

Use these repairs to locate the owning layer:

| Failure | First responsible decision |
| --- | --- |
| Every region competes | Grouping and emphasis in [composition](../visual-composition/SKILL.md) |
| Product looks interchangeable | Thesis and authentic subject in [direction](../visual-direction/SKILL.md) |
| Feedback suggests a false outcome | State contract in [interaction](../interaction-design/SKILL.md) |
| Movement feels unresponsive or confusing | Ownership, timing, and anchors in [motion](../motion-intelligence/SKILL.md) |
| Object is recognizable but implausible | Dimensions and construction in [geometry](../geometric-reasoning/SKILL.md) |
| Repeated return leaves gaps | Canonical relationships in [object structure](../object-structure/SKILL.md) |
| Parts move through obstructions | Dependencies and clearance in [assembly](../assembly-choreography/SKILL.md) |
| Narrow view hides the subject or action | Space allocation in [responsive composition](../responsive-composition/SKILL.md) |
| Output is flat, noisy, washed out, or unstable | Source isolation in [rendering judgment](../rendering-judgment/SKILL.md) |

## Refine with an interpretable comparison

State the expected visible improvement, change the smallest causal group that can produce it, and inspect the same state under matching conditions. Revisit adjacent relationships affected by a shared rule. Preserve successful decisions instead of redesigning the whole experience after every critique.

If the change adds cost without the expected benefit, undo that local change or revise the diagnosis. Repeated ineffective polish suggests that the responsible layer has not been found.

When plausible causes compete, use the smallest available diagnostic view that separates them: a neutral surface for form, fixed framing for proportion, a still state for composition, or an isolated motion event for timing. Return to the full intended presentation after diagnosis; a simplified view is evidence about its isolated question, not a substitute for the finished result.

Resolve composition, subject scale, silhouette, hierarchy, and major illumination before optical alignment, small edge treatment, minor texture variation, or temporal micro-polish. Finish should make the chosen form and behavior clearer.

## Stop honestly

For an improvement request, deliver the corrected artifact rather than stopping at this rubric. For a critique request, deliver prioritized findings tied to visible evidence. Complete the requested work when material defects in scope are resolved and available rendered observations support the result. Keep any useful evidence concise: artifact or view, relevant state and conditions, observed defect, correction, and result. Create separate records only when the project needs them.

If rendering, playback, or required inspection is unavailable, complete unaffected work and identify precisely what remains unobserved. Do not invent captures, user studies, compliance, performance measurements, or cross-agent improvement. Distinguish completed construction from pending visual acceptance.
